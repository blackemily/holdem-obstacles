The Four Obstacles in Real-World Play

The framework is useful because it travels with you from one game to another. Cash games and tournaments create different pressures, but players in both formats must evaluate position, pressure, hand strength, and risk. Opponents also differ, and the way they respond to bets can change the best approach.

19♠♦♣♥POSITION · PRESSURE · HAND STRENGTH · RISK

The challenge

The framework applies in both cash games and tournaments, but the surrounding conditions can differ. Cash-game stacks may be replenished, while tournament chips have survival and payout implications. The same hand and bet size can therefore carry different consequences depending on the format and stage of play.

Look beyond the obvious

Opponents also differ. Some players enter many pots; others are selective. Some apply pressure frequently; others show strength mainly with strong hands. Observe repeated behavior rather than drawing sweeping conclusions from one unusual action. Use those observations to refine your estimates, while remaining open to new information.

A more disciplined approach

Your own condition matters too. Fatigue, frustration, impatience, or a desire to recover losses can distort how you assess pressure and risk. Sometimes the best strategic adjustment is to take a break rather than continue making decisions while distracted.

Put the idea into practice

After a session, identify one recurring situation that challenged you. Record the four obstacles involved and one adjustment you can test next time.

Chapter takeaway
Identify the obstacle, evaluate the situation with the information available, and choose a deliberate response rather than reacting automatically.
