Part Four — Obstacle Three: Hand Strength · Chapter 10

The Problems Created by Misjudging Hand Strength

Overvaluing a hand is one of the easiest ways to become committed to a losing situation. High cards, a big pair, or a promising draw can create confidence that is no longer justified by the board or the action. The danger grows when a player becomes emotionally attached to the hand and treats every new card as a reason to continue.

10♠♦♣♥POSITION · PRESSURE · HAND STRENGTH · RISK

The challenge

Overvaluing attractive starting cards is a frequent source of trouble. High cards can look impressive, but they may miss the board or make only a vulnerable pair. A hand that begins with promise can become a weak bluff-catcher after several streets of action.

Look beyond the obvious

The opposite mistake is failing to recognize when a hand improves or when an opponent’s likely range has missed. Players who focus only on their own cards may ignore the board texture and the possible combinations available to others. The board is shared information; it must be part of every evaluation.

A more disciplined approach

Attachment is another danger. After investing chips or making a strong hand on the flop, a player may feel committed to seeing the hand through. But the turn and river can change the relative strength. Previous investment does not make a current call profitable.

Put the idea into practice

Take a hand where you lost with a pair or strong draw. Reconstruct the board street by street and write down how your assessment should have changed after each card.

Chapter takeaway
Identify the obstacle, evaluate the situation with the information available, and choose a deliberate response rather than reacting automatically.
