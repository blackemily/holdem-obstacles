From Recognizing Obstacles to Overcoming Them

Recognizing a mistake is valuable only when it changes what you do next time. Improvement comes from building a process that can be repeated, reviewed, and refined. That means learning from hands without allowing frustration or embarrassment to take control.

18♠♦♣♥POSITION · PRESSURE · HAND STRENGTH · RISK

The challenge

Recognition is the beginning of improvement, not the finish. Once you identify an obstacle, decide what habit would help you handle it better. Positional problems may call for tighter early-position choices. Pressure problems may call for a more thoughtful response to bet sizing. Hand-strength problems may require reassessment on every street. Risk problems may require more attention to pot odds and stack depth.

Look beyond the obvious

Make the habit specific enough to practice. “Play better” is too vague. “Before entering from early position, consider who acts behind me” is actionable. “Do not call a river bet until I can name enough hands I beat” is also actionable.

A more disciplined approach

Review should focus on the quality of the decision using information available at the time, not only whether the hand was won. A correct decision can lose, and a poor decision can sometimes get lucky. Separating process from outcome helps you learn consistently.

Put the idea into practice

Choose one obstacle that repeatedly affects your play. Set one measurable behavior to practice in your next session, then review whether you followed it.

Chapter takeaway
Identify the obstacle, evaluate the situation with the information available, and choose a deliberate response rather than reacting automatically.
