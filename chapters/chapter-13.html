The Problems Created by Poor Risk Management

Poor risk management often appears as a series of small justifications: one more call, one more street, one more attempt to recover chips. A player may ignore pot size, stack depth, or the likelihood of improving because the hand has already consumed so much attention and money.

13♠♦♣♥POSITION · PRESSURE · HAND STRENGTH · RISK

The challenge

One form of poor risk management is committing a large portion of a stack with a marginal hand. Another is calling a bet without comparing the price to the pot and the chance of winning. In both cases, the player focuses on wanting to continue rather than on whether continuing is justified.

Look beyond the obvious

Pot odds provide one useful comparison: the amount you must call relative to the pot you could win. Expected value broadens the question by considering the possible outcomes and their probabilities. These concepts do not remove uncertainty, but they help make the cost of a decision visible.

A more disciplined approach

Tournament risk has additional consequences. Losing a large portion of your stack can reduce future options, and elimination ends your opportunity to continue. That does not mean you should play timidly; it means stack size and tournament circumstances belong in the decision.

Put the idea into practice

Review a large losing pot. Was the risk reasonable based on what you knew at the time, or did the size of your previous investment pull you into a decision you would otherwise avoid?

Chapter takeaway
Identify the obstacle, evaluate the situation with the information available, and choose a deliberate response rather than reacting automatically.
