Part Six — Bringing the Four Obstacles Together · Chapter 15

How the Four Obstacles Interact

The four obstacles rarely appear one at a time. Position changes how much information you have. That information affects how you interpret pressure. Pressure influences the range of hands you expect. Your hand strength then affects whether the risk of continuing is justified. A decision that ignores these connections is likely to be incomplete.

The challenge

The four obstacles are most useful when considered together. Position affects how much information you receive. That information helps you interpret pressure. Pressure and board texture influence how you evaluate hand strength. Hand strength, pot size, and stack depth all affect the risk of continuing.

Look beyond the obvious

Imagine holding a medium-strength hand in late position. Several opponents check, and you face a modest bet from one player. Your position gives you information about the action; the bet creates pressure; the board determines how your hand compares with possible holdings; and the pot odds help measure the risk. No single factor tells the whole story.

A more disciplined approach

Sometimes one obstacle is clearly dominant. In other situations, two or more combine to make a decision difficult. A strong hand can still face unacceptable risk against a very narrow, powerful range. A favorable price can still be unattractive when your draw is unlikely to be best if it completes.

Put the idea into practice

Practice describing a decision through all four lenses. This prevents you from allowing one appealing feature—such as a strong starting hand or late position—to obscure the rest of the situation.

Chapter takeaway
Identify the obstacle, evaluate the situation with the information available, and choose a deliberate response rather than reacting automatically.
