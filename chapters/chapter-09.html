Why Hand Strength Is an Obstacle

A hand is not strong simply because its cards look attractive. Its value depends on the community cards, the betting, the number of opponents, and the hands those opponents could reasonably hold. A pair that looked excellent before the flop may become vulnerable on a coordinated board.

09♠♦♣♥POSITION · PRESSURE · HAND STRENGTH · RISK

The challenge

A hand does not have a fixed value throughout a Hold’em hand. Before the flop, you evaluate two private cards and their potential. After the flop, those cards interact with five community cards that are revealed over time. The same pair can be strong on one board and vulnerable on another.

Look beyond the obvious

It is important to distinguish a hand’s absolute category from its relative strength. A pair is still one pair, but its practical value depends on the board, the number of opponents, the action, and the hands opponents may hold. A completed flush or straight can change the situation dramatically.

A more disciplined approach

Draws also require careful evaluation. A drawing hand may have potential, but potential is not the same as a made hand. You must consider how often the draw will complete, whether the resulting hand is likely to be best, and what it costs to continue.

Put the idea into practice

At each new community card, reassess the situation from the beginning. Do not let the strength you believed you had on the previous street determine the next decision.

Chapter takeaway
Identify the obstacle, evaluate the situation with the information available, and choose a deliberate response rather than reacting automatically.
