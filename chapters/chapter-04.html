The Problems Created by Poor Positional Understanding

Many positional mistakes happen before the flop. A player sees attractive cards and enters the pot without considering how many opponents remain to act. A raise from behind then forces a difficult decision, or the player enters a multiway pot from a seat where future decisions will be made first.

04♠♦♣♥POSITION · PRESSURE · HAND STRENGTH · RISK

The challenge

One common positional mistake is entering too many pots from early position. When several players remain to act, you face a greater chance of a raise or a multiway pot. A hand that might have value against one opponent can become difficult when multiple players continue.

Look beyond the obvious

Another mistake is treating position as if it matters only before the flop. It continues to shape every betting round. If you act first on the flop, turn, or river, you may have to reveal information through your action before seeing what opponents do. If you act last, their checks and bets can help narrow the possibilities.

A more disciplined approach

Players also sometimes confuse being involved in a pot with having a reason to continue. Position can make a decision easier, but it cannot justify ignoring poor hand strength or excessive risk. Nor should a player assume that late position automatically makes every hand profitable.

Put the idea into practice

When reviewing a hand, note your seat and the number of players still to act. Look for places where you entered a pot without considering how difficult the next betting round might become.

Chapter takeaway
Identify the obstacle, evaluate the situation with the information available, and choose a deliberate response rather than reacting automatically.
