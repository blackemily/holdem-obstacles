Overcoming the Pressure Obstacle

The answer to pressure is not to become passive or aggressive in every situation. It is to respond deliberately. Consider the opponent, the betting sequence, your position, the board, and the strength of your hand. Then decide whether continuing, raising, or folding best fits the evidence.

08♠♦♣♥POSITION · PRESSURE · HAND STRENGTH · RISK

The challenge

Responding well to pressure begins with slowing down enough to evaluate the wager. Compare the bet with the pot, consider the likely range of hands that would make it, and examine how the board interacts with those hands. Do not decide solely because the opponent appears confident or because the bet is large.

Look beyond the obvious

Sometimes resisting pressure means calling or raising with a hand that performs well against the opponent’s likely range. Sometimes it means folding and refusing to invest more chips in a situation that has become unfavorable. The disciplined response is the one supported by the available information, not the one that feels toughest.

A more disciplined approach

You can also apply pressure when the circumstances support it. Position, a credible range, favorable board texture, and an opponent’s tendencies may create an opportunity. Aggression without a reason is not strategy; it is simply putting more chips into the pot.

Put the idea into practice

After a hand, ask whether you responded to the opponent’s action or merely reacted to it. Identify one piece of information that should guide your next similar decision.

Chapter takeaway
Identify the obstacle, evaluate the situation with the information available, and choose a deliberate response rather than reacting automatically.
