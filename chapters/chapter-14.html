Overcoming the Risk Obstacle

Managing risk does not mean avoiding every uncertain situation. It means evaluating the price of continuing and the reward available. Pot odds, expected value, stack sizes, and the likely range of outcomes help turn a vague feeling into a more structured decision.

14♠♦♣♥POSITION · PRESSURE · HAND STRENGTH · RISK

The challenge

Start by calculating the price of continuing. How much must you call, and what will the pot be if you do? Compare that price with your estimate of the chance that your hand is best or will improve. When raising, consider not only the amount you add but also how often the raise can achieve its intended result.

Look beyond the obvious

Effective stack size matters because it determines how much can be won or lost between you and the opponent. A decision involving a small fraction of the stack is different from one that risks the entire stack. Think ahead about what future bets may require if you continue now.

A more disciplined approach

Risk management is not simply protecting chips. Passing up a profitable opportunity can also be costly. The objective is to commit chips when the situation justifies it and avoid allowing fear, excitement, or previous losses to make the decision for you.

Put the idea into practice

Before a major commitment, explain the decision in one sentence: what are you risking, what can you win, and why is the price acceptable?

Chapter takeaway
Identify the obstacle, evaluate the situation with the information available, and choose a deliberate response rather than reacting automatically.
