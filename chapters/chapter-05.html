Overcoming the Position Obstacle

Position cannot be changed during a hand, but your decisions can adapt to it. A disciplined player is more selective when acting early and looks for opportunities to use the information advantage of later position. The goal is not to play every hand from the button or fold everything from early position. It is to understand how position changes the quality of each opportunity.

05♠♦♣♥POSITION · PRESSURE · HAND STRENGTH · RISK

The challenge

A practical positional strategy begins before you look for reasons to play a hand. Know where you are seated and consider how many opponents remain to act. Be more selective when you will have to make decisions early, and recognize that later position can provide more opportunities to enter or contest a pot.

Look beyond the obvious

Use position to plan the hand rather than treating it as a label. If you enter from early position, think ahead: what will you do if you face a raise, a caller, or a difficult flop? If you enter from late position, consider how the players before you have acted and whether you can use that information to make a clearer decision.

A more disciplined approach

Position is an advantage, not a guarantee. A late seat does not erase a weak holding, and an early seat does not automatically require a fold. Combine position with the quality of your hand, the action, and the price of continuing.

Put the idea into practice

During a session, make a simple note whenever you enter a pot. Record your position and whether the decision became easier or harder because of it. Patterns will emerge.

Chapter takeaway
Identify the obstacle, evaluate the situation with the information available, and choose a deliberate response rather than reacting automatically.
